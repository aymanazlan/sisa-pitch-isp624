Solution

Haziq45 s · end by 4:05

Key line: Report, verify, merge, prioritise, track.

Thank you, Aiman.
Our answer is SISA.
One photo, five steps.
Report: take a photo, and the app saves the location.
Verify: AI checks if it’s public waste, and how bad it is.
In our prototype, this part is simulated.
Merge: same rubbish, one hotspot, one confirmation per person.
Prioritise: three residents, or three days, and it turns urgent.
Track: residents follow it until it’s cleared, and earn EcoPoints.

Delivery: Count the five steps on your fingers. Say each one as it lights up in the simulation.

Likely questions

Is the AI real? Najib

Not yet. The AI scan is simulated, and we say so on the slides. Everything after the photo works: merging, priority, alerts, tracking and EcoPoints. A real image model is our next step.

SISPAA and PBTCare already exist. Why another app? Haziq

They collect reports one by one. SISA adds what happens after: one hotspot, automatic priority, and tracking. And 39% of non-reporters don’t even know where to report.

How do you stop fake or spam reports? Haziq

Every report needs a photo and a location. The AI checks it. And each account can confirm a hotspot only once.

What about privacy? Aiman

The app only needs the rubbish photo and its location. Nothing else about the resident. We will review privacy in the pilot.

Why would residents keep using it? Aiman

Residents told us: recognition, progress updates and rewards. SISA gives tracking and EcoPoints, so they see their report made a difference.

Who pays for it? Syazwan

Residents use it free. Township management, councils and residents’ associations pay for the dashboard. 63.6% of residents supported that.

What would you do differently? Aiman

Talk to users first. We built fast, but our evidence came after the hackathon. Next time, we start with residents on day one.
