06

Solution

Haziq68 s · end by 5:30

Thank you, Aiman. Our answer is SISA. It works in five steps, on the phone residents already have. One, report: snap one photo, and the app records the location. Two, verify: AI checks the waste type, how serious it is, and whether it is on public land. Three, merge: if another resident reports the same rubbish, it joins the same hotspot, and each account can confirm only once. Four, prioritise: when three residents confirm it, or it stays for three days, it turns URGENT and management is alerted. Five, track: residents follow the case until it is resolved, and earn EcoPoints for helping. On the right, you can see this happening in a residential park. So instead of a pile of complaints, management gets one clear case, with a location and a priority. And the resident sees that one photo made a difference.

Likely questions

Is the AI real? Najib

Not yet. In the prototype the AI scan is simulated, and we say so on the slides. Everything after the photo is working: merging, priority, alerts, tracking and EcoPoints. Connecting a real image model is our next step.

SISPAA and PBTCare already exist. Why build another app? Haziq

Those channels collect reports one by one. SISA adds what happens after the report: merging duplicates into one hotspot, raising priority automatically, and letting residents track the case. And 39.4% of our non-reporters do not even know where to report.

How do you stop fake or spam reports? Haziq

Each report needs a photo and a location, the AI check verifies the waste type and public land, and each account can confirm a hotspot only once. 52.3% of residents raised fake reports as a worry, so this is built into the design, and we will test it in the pilot.

What about privacy? Aiman

56.8% of residents were worried about privacy. The app only needs the rubbish photo and its location, and residents report from their own accounts. A privacy review is part of the pilot.

Why would residents keep using it? Aiman

Residents told us what motivates them: recognition (59.1%), progress updates (56.8%) and rewards (50%). SISA gives case tracking and EcoPoints, so residents can see that their report led to action.

Who pays for it? Syazwan

Residents use the app for free. Township and property management companies, councils and residents’ associations would subscribe to the admin dashboard. 63.6% of residents supported management paying, including all four committee members we surveyed.

What would you do differently at the hackathon? Aiman

Talk to users first. We built a working app in one week, but our evidence came only after the hackathon, from the survey. Next time we would collect a few resident interviews on day one and build the pitch around them.
